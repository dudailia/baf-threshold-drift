# Post hoc results

Everything in this file is post hoc: the analyses were declared in PROTOCOL.md ("Post hoc analyses") after the main results at commit `125042c` had been seen. They do not change any main-study result, the main RESULTS.md numbers, PREDICTIONS.md or the prediction scoring. Every run is reported, including null results. Numbers are generated by `src/trace.py` from `results_posthoc/` (and, where a main-study number is quoted, from `results/`); choices made without the user are in reports/decisions.md. Conventions as in RESULTS.md: budget miss is realized FPR minus 5%, TPR is shown next to realized FPR, the oracle is an upper bound and not deployable, seed spread (5 seeds) and seed 0 bootstrap intervals (1,000 paired resamples) are reported separately, and "differs" means a paired 95% interval that excludes 0 with marginal intervals that overlap by less than half the shorter one.

Reading. This is a null result for single features: no one feature among the top 10, replaced on its own, accounts for the negatives' score drift. One-at-a-time replacement breaks the joint distribution of the features, so it can widen the miss (as for `housing_status`), and drift spread over several features or carried by their joint distribution would not show up here. It also qualifies the main RESULTS.md sentence that the `credit_risk_score` drift is "consistent with" the static misses: that feature's drift, replaced alone, does not explain them.

Its TPR is {{value:retrain/tpr_minus_lag1/min|pt2}} to {{value:retrain/tpr_minus_lag1/max|pt2}} points from delayed-label lag 1's.

Reading. Retraining with a threshold from the previous month does not keep the alert budget better than the label-free validation-rate strategy: it inherits the month-to-month swings of delayed-label lag 1. What it adds is detection, a few points of TPR over lag 1 at a similar budget miss, consistent with a model trained on more recent months. It does not recover Variant V's loss after month 5 (the training months still carry the injected bias).

## 5. Probability-based label-free targeting (exploratory)

Isotonic regression maps main-study LightGBM scores to fraud probability p, fitted on month 3 only. In each test month the threshold flags the largest set whose estimated FPR, the sum of (1 - p) over flagged applications divided by the sum over all applications, is at most 5%; no test-month labels are used. In the bootstrap, month 3 is resampled and the isotonic map refitted in each resample. Comparisons are paired on the same resampled rows (seed 0).

{{table:isotonic}}

Isotonic targeting is within 0.5 points of budget in {{value:isotonic/n_within_0.5pt|int}} of {{value:isotonic/n|int}} variant-months (miss {{value:isotonic/miss/min|pt2}} to {{value:isotonic/miss/max|pt2}} points). On absolute miss it is smaller than label-free top 5% in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_top/n_differs_smaller|int}} of {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_top/n|int}}, smaller than static in {{value:isotonic/vs/static/n_differs_smaller|int}} (larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/static/n_differs_larger|int}}), smaller than delayed-label lag 1 in {{value:isotonic/vs/delayed_lag1/n_differs_smaller|int}} (larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/delayed_lag1/n_differs_larger|int}}), and against label-free at the validation alert rate smaller in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_differs_smaller|int}}, larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_differs_larger|int}} and inconclusive in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_inconclusive|int}}.

Reading. Estimating each month's FPR from calibrated probabilities removes most of the overshoot that the fixed validation alert rate shows where the fraud rate falls (Variants III to V), because it does not assume the fraud rate stays at month 3's; in Base, I and II, where the validation-rate strategy was already close, it is slightly further under budget. This is post hoc and exploratory, chosen after the main results were seen, on one dataset; it is a candidate for a pre-registered test, not a finding to rank above the main study's strategies.

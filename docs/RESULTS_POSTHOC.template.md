# Post hoc results

Everything in this file is post hoc: the analyses were declared in PROTOCOL.md ("Post hoc analyses") after the main results at commit `125042c` had been seen. They do not change any main-study result, the main RESULTS.md numbers, PREDICTIONS.md or the prediction scoring. Every run is reported, including null results. Numbers are generated by `src/trace.py` from `results_posthoc/` (and, where a main-study number is quoted, from `results/`); choices made without the user are in reports/decisions.md. Conventions as in RESULTS.md: budget miss is realized FPR minus 5%, TPR is shown next to realized FPR, the oracle is an upper bound and not deployable, seed spread (5 seeds) and seed 0 bootstrap intervals (1,000 paired resamples) are reported separately, and "differs" means a paired 95% interval that excludes 0 with marginal intervals that overlap by less than half the shorter one.

It also qualifies the main RESULTS.md sentence that the `credit_risk_score` drift is "consistent with" the static misses: neither `velocity_4w` nor `credit_risk_score`, replaced alone, explains them.

## 5. Probability-based label-free targeting (exploratory)

Isotonic regression maps main-study LightGBM scores to fraud probability p, fitted on month 3 only. In each test month the threshold flags the largest set whose estimated FPR, the sum of (1 - p) over flagged applications divided by the sum over all applications, is at most 5%; no test-month labels are used. In the bootstrap, month 3 is resampled and the isotonic map refitted in each resample. Comparisons are paired on the same resampled rows (seed 0).

Isotonic targeting is within 0.5 points of budget in {{value:isotonic/n_within_0.5pt|int}} of {{value:isotonic/n|int}} variant-months (miss {{value:isotonic/miss/min|pt2}} to {{value:isotonic/miss/max|pt2}} points). On absolute miss it is smaller than label-free top 5% in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_top/n_differs_smaller|int}} of {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_top/n|int}}, smaller than static in {{value:isotonic/vs/static/n_differs_smaller|int}} (larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/static/n_differs_larger|int}}), smaller than delayed-label lag 1 in {{value:isotonic/vs/delayed_lag1/n_differs_smaller|int}} (larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/delayed_lag1/n_differs_larger|int}}), and against label-free at the validation alert rate smaller in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_differs_smaller|int}}, larger in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_differs_larger|int}} and inconclusive in {{value:isotonic/vs/label_free_val_rate/n_inconclusive|int}}.

Reading. Estimating each month's FPR from calibrated probabilities removes most of the overshoot that the fixed validation alert rate shows where the fraud rate falls (Variants III to V), because it does not assume the fraud rate stays at month 3's; in Base, I and II, where the validation-rate strategy was already close, it is slightly further under budget. This is post hoc and exploratory, chosen after the main results were seen, on one dataset; it is a candidate for a pre-registered test, not a finding to rank above the main study's strategies.

## 7. Control for the isotonic result

Fixed label-free rules that flag the top 5.0%, 5.25%, 5.5%, 5.75% and 6.0% of each month's applications, on the same main-study scores (5 seeds), next to isotonic targeting (section 5). The last column is the alert volume at which realized FPR is exactly 5%, the oracle's (an upper bound, not deployable).

{{table:fixedq}}

By the identity FPR = (alert share - TPR x prevalence) / (1 - prevalence), a month's FPR is exactly 5% at an alert share of 5% x (1 - prevalence) + TPR x prevalence; here that share ranges from {{value:fixedq/oracle_alert_rate/min|pct2}}% to {{value:fixedq/oracle_alert_rate/max|pct2}}% across variant-months. A fixed rule that flags the top 5.75% of applications, with no calibration and no labels, is within 0.5 points of budget in {{value:fixedq/top_5.75pct/n_within_0.5pt|int}} of {{value:fixedq/top_5.75pct/n|int}} variant-months, and the top 5.5% in {{value:fixedq/top_5.5pct/n_within_0.5pt|int}}, against {{value:isotonic/n_within_0.5pt|int}} for isotonic targeting; the mean absolute miss is {{value:fixedq/best_rule/mean_abs_miss|pt2}} points for the best fixed rule ({{value:fixedq/best_rule|str}}) and {{value:fixedq/isotonic/mean_abs_miss|pt2}} for isotonic. On the same resampled rows, isotonic has a smaller absolute miss than the top 5.5% rule in {{value:fixedq/top_5.5pct/iso_smaller|int}} variant-months, a larger one in {{value:fixedq/top_5.5pct/iso_larger|int}} and is inconclusive in {{value:fixedq/top_5.5pct/inconclusive|int}}.

Reading. Most of the isotonic result follows from the FPR identity at this prevalence: with fraud near 1% of applications and TPR near one half, flagging about 5.5% of applications already lands near 5% FPR, so any rule that aims at that share does nearly as well. Calibration adds a modest reduction in the average miss and adapts the share month by month, but the gain over a well-chosen fixed share is small, and choosing that share itself needs labels from some month.

## 9. What changes relative to the main study

Contradictions. The main study's README, one-pager and RESULTS.md say that the static threshold under-spends its budget in months 5 to 7. Post hoc, that holds when the threshold is set on month 3 or month 4, but a threshold set on month 2 over-spends in every later month (section 1): the finding that holds on every split is that a threshold set once misses its budget, by more than half a point in most later months, with a sign that depends on the validation month. The main RESULTS.md also called the `credit_risk_score` drift "consistent with" the static misses and said it drifted most in every variant; a later claims audit found that `velocity_4w` drifts most in four of the six variants, and replacing either feature alone does not reduce the misses (section 2). These claims were corrected in an amendment to RESULTS.md dated 2026-10-17, which quotes the original wording.

Qualifications. The main study's statement that label-free targeting to the validation alert rate holds the budget best is true among the pre-declared strategies; post hoc, isotonic estimated-FPR targeting (section 5) is within 0.5 points in every variant-month, including where the validation-rate strategy overshoots, but it was chosen after seeing the results. Retraining with a previous-month threshold (section 4) adds TPR but not budget control.

The validation month alone, with the model held fixed, also reverses the sign (section 8). Most of the isotonic result follows from the FPR identity: a fixed share of about 5.5% to 5.75% of applications does nearly as well (section 7).

Confirmations. With the threshold set on month 3, the sign of the static miss in months 5 to 7 does not depend on the tuned configuration or on strong regularisation (section 3); label-free top 5% stays under budget and delayed-label lag 1 keeps swinging on both new splits (section 1).

The main-study files, the main RESULTS.md numbers, PREDICTIONS.md and the prediction scoring are unchanged by these analyses; the main documents were reworded on 2026-10-17 (amendment at the top of RESULTS.md), with the original wording quoted there and kept in git history.

# Threshold drift and recalibration on BAF: one page

Question. A fraud model's threshold is set once to hit 5% FPR. How far do realized FPR, TPR and alert volume drift in later months, how unevenly across age groups, and which recalibration keeps the alert budget on target?

Setup. BAF (Jesus et al., NeurIPS 2022): a base dataset and five variants sampled from one generator, so the six share one base and are not independent replications. LightGBM tuned over 20 configurations on month 3, trained once on months 0 to 2, 5 seeds; logistic regression as a baseline; test months 4 to 7. That validation split is a single choice; other splits were run only post hoc. Strategies: static (threshold from month 3), label-free (top 5% of each month, or month 3's alert rate), delayed-label (threshold from labelled month m minus 1, 2 or 3), and an oracle using each test month's own labels (an upper bound, not deployable). The primary comparison is budget miss, realized FPR minus 5%, because all strategies sit on the same month's ROC curve. Paired bootstrap, 1,000 resamples. Predictions were pre-registered (7 of 15 hit).

Finding 1: a threshold set once misses its budget, and the direction depends on the validation month. Set on month 3, static under-spends by 0.87 to 2.32 points in months 5 to 7 (18 of 18 intervals below zero). Post hoc, set on month 2 it over-spends by 0.33 to 5.82 points, and with the model held fixed a threshold set on month 5 over-spends in 9 of 12 cells. The original "under-spends" claim is amended in RESULTS.md.

Finding 2: the label-free results are largely arithmetic at this prevalence. Matching month 3's alert rate stays within 0.5 points in 22 of 24 variant-months; top 5% of applications is under budget everywhere because frauds take part of the alerts. Since FPR = (alert share - TPR x prevalence) / (1 - prevalence), 5% FPR here means flagging 5.26% to 6.10% of applications, and a fixed post hoc rule flagging 5.75% is within 0.5 points in 24 of 24.

Finding 3: recalibrating a global threshold does not change the age-group disparity. The FPR ratio differs across global-threshold strategies by at most 0.094 in any variant-month. In Variant IV it stays between 0.081 and 0.125 in every test month, before and after the prevalence disparity is removed, and older applicants get about 89% of label-free alerts at 51% of applications. Group-wise thresholds (exploratory, using age at decision time) raise the ratio to 0.547 to 0.998. Where the injected bias is removed, detection falls for every strategy, the oracle included (Variant V oracle TPR 75.1% before, 33.6% after).

Limits. The shuffled-label check failed its declared tolerance (pooled TPR 0.117 against 0.05 plus or minus 0.02); diagnostics found no leak and point to a wrong null, but this stays a caveat. BAF is synthetic; one main split, no retraining in the main study, 20 tuning configurations; no single feature explains the static miss. The replication of the paper's setup lands inside the loose Figure 1 bound, and an independent recomputation matched every stored metric. The contribution is a measurement on BAF and a month-by-month strategy comparison by budget miss, not the observation that thresholds drift.

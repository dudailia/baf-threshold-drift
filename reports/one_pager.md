# Threshold drift and recalibration on BAF: one page

Question. A fraud model's threshold is set once to hit 5% FPR. How far do realized FPR, TPR and alert volume drift in later months, how unevenly across age groups, and which recalibration keeps the alert budget on target with the least loss in detection?

Setup. All six BAF variants (Jesus et al., NeurIPS 2022). LightGBM tuned over 20 configurations on validation month 3 and trained once on months 0 to 2, 5 seeds; logistic regression as a baseline. Test months 4 to 7. Strategies: static (threshold from month 3), label-free (top 5% of each month, or the month 3 alert rate), delayed-label (threshold from labelled month m minus 1, 2 or 3), and an oracle that uses each test month's own labels (an upper bound, not deployable). Primary comparison: budget miss, realized FPR minus 5%, because every strategy sits on the same month's ROC curve and overspending buys TPR. Paired bootstrap, 1,000 resamples per month; seed spread reported separately. Predictions were pre-registered before any model ran (7 of 15 hit).

Finding 1: the static threshold under-spends, and label-free recalibration to the validation alert rate holds the budget best. Static is close to budget in month 4 and then under budget by 0.87 to 2.32 points in months 5 to 7 in every variant, with all 18 seed 0 intervals below zero; logistic regression shows the same. Matching month 3's alert rate stays within 0.5 points in 22 of 24 variant-months and has the smallest miss of the deployable strategies in 18; it drifts over budget where the fraud rate falls below month 3's (up to +0.70 points in Variant V). Top 5% of applications is under budget in every cell (-0.26 to -1.06 points) because frauds take part of the alerts. Delayed-label lag 1 swings from -2.09 to +1.24 points, consistent with month-to-month shifts that do not persist.

Finding 2: where the injected bias is removed, detection falls for every strategy, the oracle included. In Variant V the oracle's TPR goes from 75.1% in months 4 and 5 to 33.6% in months 6 and 7; in Variant IV from 54.2% to 41.2%. No threshold recovers that loss, and the remaining TPR differences between strategies mostly reflect how much FPR budget each spends.

Finding 3: recalibrating a global threshold does not change the age-group disparity. Across global-threshold strategies the FPR ratio differs by at most 0.094 in any variant-month. In Variant IV the ratio stays between 0.081 and 0.125 in every test month, before and after the prevalence disparity is removed, and the older group gets about 89% of label-free alerts while making up 51% of applications. Group-wise thresholds, an exploratory diagnostic that uses age at decision time, raise the ratio to 0.547 to 0.998.

Limits. The shuffled-label check failed its declared tolerance (pooled TPR 0.117 against 0.05 plus or minus 0.02); diagnostics found no leak and point to a wrong null, since fraud applications are atypical and a model fitted to random labels can exceed 5% TPR, but this stays a caveat. BAF is synthetic and its variants share one generator; there is one split, one training run per seed, no retraining, and 20 tuning configurations. The replication of the paper's setup lands inside the loose Figure 1 bound (top-5 TPR 0.552). An independent recomputation matched every stored metric. The contribution is a measurement on BAF and a month-by-month comparison of threshold strategies by budget miss, not the observation that thresholds drift, which is already known.
